Local knowledge

A community of first roofs

San Tan Valley grew up in two big waves, the 2000s and the 2010s, when neighborhoods like Johnson Ranch, San Tan Heights, Copper Basin, Pecan Creek and Skyline Ranch filled in the desert east of Queen Creek almost overnight. Nearly every roof out here is concrete tile, and nearly all of it went on over whatever felt underlayment the builder was buying by the truckload that year.

That felt is the whole story. The tiles will outlive your mortgage, but builder-grade underlayment ages out in Arizona heat on its own schedule, and the 2000s-wave homes are reaching it now. The honest fix is a tile underlayment replacement: lift your tiles, replace the felt, relay the same tiles. Decades more roof without paying for tile you already own.

The other thing about San Tan Valley: most of you commute. You leave before sunrise, come home after dark, and nobody's staring at the roof in between. Out here the first sign of a roof problem is usually a ceiling stain, not the storm that caused it, weeks earlier.

And with open desert running right up to the San Tan Mountains, monsoon wind and dust storms arrive with nothing to slow them down. After a big one, get the roof looked at. Mine is the free kind of look.

Roof age in San Tan Valley

How old is your roof, and what does that mean?

On a concrete tile roof, age tells me more about the underlayment than about the tile. San Tan Valley filled in fast, so whole neighborhoods reach the same point at about the same time. This is roughly where each build wave sits today.

When the home was built What I usually find under the tiles What I tell the homeowner
Mid-2000s, the first big wave Roofs around 17 to 22 years old. The felt is in its failure window: brittle, cracking at valleys and around pipes first. Plan a re-felt now, on your schedule, instead of letting a monsoon pick the date.
Late 2000s to early 2010s Around 13 to 17 years. Felt drying out, edges starting to curl. Watch it. A free walk-around every couple of years means you see it coming.
Mid-2010s and newer Under about 12 years. Felt still pliable, seams intact. Nothing to replace. A leak on a roof this young is almost always flashing or a cracked tile.
Bought resale and not sure Could be any of the above. I lift tiles in several spots to find out. A free roof inspection in San Tan Valley answers it in one visit.

Hiring a roofer in San Tan Valley

How to choose a roofing contractor out here

San Tan Valley gets a lot of door knockers after every monsoon. Whoever you hire, these are the five things I'd check on my own house.

  1. Verify the license. Look the company up on the Arizona Registrar of Contractors site. Mine is ROC #325377.
  2. Ask who is on the roof. A salesman gets paid when you buy a roof. The person doing the work gets paid when the roof stops leaking. Those are different jobs.
  3. Get the bid line by line. Tear-off, underlayment, flashing, disposal and permit should each be visible. Here's what a roof replacement costs in Arizona, so you can read one.
  4. Make them name the underlayment. On a tile roof, the underlayment is most of what you're buying. "Standard underlayment" is not an answer. My notes on the best underlayment for a tile roof in Arizona cover the options.
  5. Ask what happens after the deposit. You should be talking to the same person before and after you sign.

My San Tan Valley home was built in the 2000s. Why is the roof having problems already?

Most San Tan Valley roofs are concrete tile over builder-grade felt underlayment, and Arizona heat wears out that felt long before the tiles. The tile is fine. Do I need a roof replacement or just a repair?

Usually just a repair. If the tiles are sound and the felt underneath is still pliable, a repair is the right call. If the felt has failed across the roof, a tile underlayment replacement reuses your tiles and costs far less than a new roof. A full roof replacement in San Tan Valley makes sense when the roofing itself is worn out, which is more common on shingle than on tile.
