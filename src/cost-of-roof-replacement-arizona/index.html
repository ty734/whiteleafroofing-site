What moves the number

The factors that actually set your price

When I bid a roof, these are the things I'm actually pricing:

  • 01Roof size. The single biggest factor. More squares means more material and more labor, plain and simple.
  • 02Material. Tile, shingle, and foam all price differently. More on each below.
  • 03Underlayment choice. On tile roofs the underlayment is the part doing the real work, and you pick the grade. It's the quietest lever on both price and lifespan.
  • 04Wood repairs. Dry rot and hidden damage under the old roof have to be fixed before the new one goes on. I include this honestly instead of springing it on you later.
  • 05Pitch and complexity. Steep roofs and rooflines with lots of ridges, valleys, and elevations take more time and more safety setup.
  • 06Solar panels, permits, and disposal. Panels have to come off and go back on, permits have to be pulled, and the old roof has to be hauled away. All part of the bid.

The quiet decision

Underlayment: where your money actually goes

On a tile roof, the tiles are mostly there to protect the underlayment, and the underlayment is what actually keeps water out of your house. It's also the part that fails first in Arizona heat.

When I bid your roof, I bring real underlayment samples and lay out the options side by side: what each grade costs and how it holds up. You pick the price and quality point. That's not a sales technique, it's just how I'd want to buy a roof myself.

Two bids for the "same roof" can be thousands of dollars apart purely because of the underlayment spec buried in the fine print. If you're comparing quotes, that's the first line to check.

If your tiles are fine and it's only the underlayment that's done, the numbers are different again. I broke those down in my tile roof underlayment replacement cost guide.

Roofing estimates in Arizona

How to compare roofing quotes, line by line

Most homeowners collect two or three roofing estimates and compare the number at the bottom. The number at the bottom is the least useful line on the page. Two bids for "the same roof" are often not bidding the same roof. Put them side by side and ask these questions of every one, mine included.

Line item What to ask Why it matters
Who priced the roof Did the person writing the bid actually walk the roof? Underlayment condition and soft decking do not show from the driveway. I walk every roof myself before I give a real number.
Underlayment Is the product named, or does the bid just say "underlayment"? On a tile roof this is the part keeping water out, and grades differ a lot in price. It is the most common reason two bids are thousands apart.
Wood and decking repair Is rotted wood included, or billed separately after tear-off? An open-ended wood line is how a low bid becomes a high invoice. My quotes include anything unexpected that comes up during the job.
Tear-off and disposal Is removing and hauling away the old roof in the price? It is real labor and real dumpster fees. If it is missing, the bid is not complete.
Permits Who pulls the permit, and is the fee included? Requirements vary by city. The Chandler and Gilbert permit and code rules are a good example of what is involved.
Solar panels Is removing and resetting the panels included? Panels have to come off for the roof to be done right. More on that in replacing a roof with solar panels.
Flashing and valley metal Is the metal being replaced, or reused from the old roof? Flashing and valleys are where a lot of Arizona leaks start. Ask so you know what you are paying for.
Final price Is this number final, or can change orders add to it? A low bid with open-ended extras is not actually low.
License What is the ROC number? Check it yourself on the Arizona ROC contractor search. Mine is ROC #325377.

Cost questions

What homeowners ask me about price

How much does a new roof cost in Arizona?

Most roof replacements in Arizona land between $8,000 and $20,000. By home size: under 3,000 square feet usually runs $8,000 to $15,000, homes between 3,000 and 4,000 square feet run $14,000 to $20,000, and homes over 4,000 square feet run $18,000 to $30,000. Material, underlayment grade, and the condition of the wood move the final number, which is why every roof gets a free individual bid.

How much does roof repair cost in Arizona?

Most repairs run $500 to $3,500, depending on the damage to the tiles or shingles and to the wood underneath. Past that range, replacement usually makes more sense and I'll show you the math.

How much does a new roof cost in Chandler?

The same as anywhere else I work in the East Valley. I use one set of price ranges across Chandler, Gilbert, Mesa, Queen Creek, San Tan Valley, Apache Junction, Sun Lakes and Gold Canyon. What moves a Chandler bid is the roof itself: most homes here are concrete tile, and whether the existing tile can be reused is often the biggest swing in the price. There is more on the Chandler roof replacement page.

What's the cheapest way to deal with an aging tile roof?

If the tiles are in good shape, a tile underlayment replacement. We reuse your existing tiles and replace only the failed underlayment beneath them. It's one of the most common jobs we do in the East Valley for exactly this reason.

Why do bids vary so much between roofing companies?

Overhead, materials, and scope. Check the underlayment grade being quoted, whether wood repairs are included, and whether the number is final. Two bids that look thousands apart are often not bidding the same roof.

How do I compare roofing estimates in Arizona?

Line by line, not by the total. Check whether the person who wrote the bid walked the roof, which underlayment is named, whether wood repair, tear-off, disposal, permits and solar panel removal are included, whether flashing and valley metal are new, whether the price is final, and whether the ROC license number checks out.

How long does a roof replacement take?

Most of my roof replacements take 4 to 5 days from tear-off to cleanup. Bigger or more complicated roofs, and any wood repair found under the old roof, can add to that.

Does homeowners insurance pay for a new roof in Arizona?

It depends on the cause and on your policy. Sudden storm damage, like monsoon wind, haboobs and hail, is typically the kind of event homeowners policies cover. A roof that simply wore out with age typically is not. Your own policy controls, so read it for your deductible and coverage type. The full process is on my insurance claims page.
