What the free inspection is

I walk the roof. All of it.

A real roof inspection isn't a guy glancing at your house from the driveway. When I inspect a roof, I get up on it and check everything that actually fails on Arizona homes:

  • 01Tiles. Cracked, slipped or loose tiles, and whether your existing tile can be reused if the roof ever needs work.
  • 02Underlayment. I pull tiles in several spots to see the felt underneath. This is what actually decides repair vs. replace on most East Valley roofs.
  • 03Flashing and penetrations. Valleys, vents, skylights and anywhere a pipe comes through the roof. Most leaks start here.
  • 04Fascia and eaves. Dry rot and water damage at the edges, the early warning signs most homeowners never see.

Then I come down and explain what I found in plain English. No jargon, no scare tactics, no invoice. If it needs work, you'll get a clear bid with options. If it doesn't, I'll tell you that too.

My roof inspection checklist, and what each finding usually means

Here's what I'm looking at up there and how I read it. The right-hand column is the part most homeowners never get told.

What I check What I'm looking for What it usually means
Tiles or shingles Cracked, slipped or missing tiles. Curling, cracked or bald shingles. A handful of bad tiles is a repair. Worn shingles across the whole roof point toward replacement.
Underlayment I lift tiles in several spots to see whether the felt underneath is still flexible or has gone brittle and torn. This decides repair vs. replace on most East Valley tile roofs. Good tile over failed felt is a tile underlayment replacement, not a new roof.
Valleys and flashing Debris packed in valleys, rusted or lifted metal, gaps where the roof meets a wall. Where a lot of leaks start. Often a targeted fix if it's caught before water reaches the wood.
Penetrations Vents, pipe boots, skylights and satellite mounts, and whether their seals have cracked in the sun. Usually a small repair. Worth doing before monsoon season rather than after.
Fascia and eaves Dry rot, soft wood and water staining along the edges. An early warning that water has been getting in for a while. Wood repair gets folded into whatever fix the roof needs.
Flat and foam sections Ponding water, worn coating, blisters, and exposed foam. A worn coating is a recoat. Foam that's broken down is a bigger job. More on foam roofing.
Storm damage Wind-lifted tiles along ridges and hips, and debris driven under tiles after a monsoon or haboob. If it's sudden storm damage, I'll tell you whether it's worth an insurance claim. If it isn't, I'll tell you that too.

What do you check during a roof inspection?

Tiles or shingles, the underlayment under the tiles (I lift tiles in several spots to see it), valleys and flashing, every penetration such as vents, pipe boots, skylights and satellite mounts, the fascia and eaves, any flat or foam sections, and signs of monsoon or haboob damage.

How often should I get my roof inspected in Arizona?

Before monsoon season is the smart window, and again after any big storm or haboob. If your tile roof is 15 years or older, it is time, because that is the age where the underlayment starts to fail even when the tiles still look fine from the street.
