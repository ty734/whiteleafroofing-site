The Arizona tile roof problem

Tiles last 50 years. Felt doesn't.

Concrete and clay tile is the perfect Arizona roofing material. It shrugs off UV, handles monsoon rain, and often lasts 50 years or more. But tile isn't actually the waterproof layer of your roof. The real waterproofing is the underlayment, the felt membrane between the tiles and the wood deck, and in our heat it typically gives out at 15 to 20 years.

Summer after summer of heat cycling bakes the felt brittle. It cracks, tears and slides, and once that happens, monsoon water gets past the tiles and finds your decking, your insulation, and eventually your ceiling. From the street, the roof looks perfect the whole time.

Most of the East Valley was built in the 1990s and 2000s, which means entire neighborhoods in Chandler and Gilbert are sitting on original underlayment that's past its service life right now. It's the single most common problem I find on inspections.

If you are still working out what this layer even is, I wrote a plain guide to what roof underlayment is and why it matters in Arizona. This page is about the job itself: replacing it.

The timing question

When a re-felt is actually due

This is the question I get more than any other, and the honest answer is that roof age tells you most of it. Felt underlayment on an East Valley tile roof generally has 15 to 20 years in it. Not because it wears out from rain, we barely get any, but because it spends two decades baking. A west-facing slope with no shade gets there sooner than a north slope. Original builder-grade felt gets there sooner than anything I would put down today.

Roof age What I usually find under the tiles What I tell the homeowner
Under 10 years Felt still pliable, seams intact Nothing to do. If there is a leak on a roof this young, it is almost always flashing or a cracked tile, not the underlayment.
10 to 15 years Drying out, edges starting to curl Watch it. Take the free walk-around every couple of years so you see this coming instead of finding out during a monsoon.
15 to 20 years The failure window. Brittle felt, cracking at valleys and penetrations first This is when I start recommending a re-felt, and when planning it beats reacting to it. You pick the timing instead of the weather picking it for you.
20 to 25 years Felt tearing where the roof was walked, black grit in the gutters Overdue. Every monsoon from here is a coin flip, and water that gets in stops being an underlayment job and starts being a decking job.
25 years or more on original felt Felt effectively gone in places, wood often stained Replace it, and budget for some wood repair. I will show you photos of what I found before I quote a dollar of it.

Age is the starting point, not the verdict. The four things that actually move a roof up the list are water stains on a ceiling after monsoon rain, black grit and felt fragments washing into the gutters, tiles that have slipped out of line, and daylight visible from inside the attic. Any one of those and I would rather look at it now than in August.

Underlayment questions

What homeowners ask me

What is roof underlayment replacement?

It is the job of replacing the waterproof membrane underneath your tile, without replacing the tile. My crew lifts and stacks your existing tiles, strips the failed felt, repairs any bad decking, lays new underlayment, and relays your same tile. In the trade we call it a re-felt and relay. From the street the roof looks unchanged, and the layer that actually keeps water out of your house is brand new.

How much does tile roof underlayment replacement cost?

It depends on roof size, the underlayment you choose, and how many tiles need swapping, so every job gets a free individual bid. The headline is that reusing your existing tiles costs far less than a full roof replacement, because the most expensive material on the roof stays right where it is.

Why does underlayment fail when the tiles look fine?

Tiles often last 50 years or more here, but the felt beneath them typically gives out at 15 to 20 years. Arizona heat cycling bakes it brittle, and once it cracks, the tiles alone won't keep water out of your house.

Can you really reuse my existing tiles?

Usually, yes. If the tiles are in good shape we lift them carefully, replace the underlayment, swap any cracked tiles, and relay your same tile. The roof looks the way it always has, with brand new waterproofing underneath.

How often should roof underlayment be replaced in Arizona?

Plan on 15 to 20 years for felt on an East Valley tile roof. Sun exposure is what sets the pace, so a roof with no shade and a lot of west-facing slope lands at the short end of that. Tiles are not the clock. They often go 50 years or more, which is exactly why so many houses here need underlayment work while the roof still looks perfect.

Do I have to do the whole roof at once?

Not always. If one slope is failing and the rest genuinely has years left, I will say so and we can do that section. But felt on the same roof was installed on the same day and has been under the same sun, so partial work is usually a way of paying mobilization costs twice. I will tell you honestly which situation you are in.

Is underlayment replacement the same as a re-roof?

People use the words loosely, so it is worth pinning down. A re-felt and relay reuses your existing tile and replaces what is under it. A re-roof usually means new roofing material going on. Both are different from a full tear-off replacement. I walk through the differences on my roof replacement versus reroofing page.

Will my homeowners insurance pay for it?

Generally no, and I would rather you hear that from me before you file. Insurers cover sudden damage from a storm, not a layer that wore out on schedule. If a monsoon actually tore your roof up, that is a different conversation and I can help with the documentation, which I cover on my roof insurance claims page.

Which underlayment should I choose?

I bring physical samples of several options to the estimate so you can compare them side by side. Manufacturer warranties depend on the material you choose. My written guide to the best underlayment for Arizona tile roofs covers the trade-offs.

How do I know if my underlayment is failing?

Water stains after monsoon rain, felt debris in the gutters, and simple age. If your home was built in the 1990s or 2000s and still has original underlayment, it's in the failure window. A repair inspection settles it either way, free.

Is the inspection really free?

Yes. I walk the roof personally and tell you honestly whether the underlayment is failing or has years left. I told Roger Edwards his roof was good for several more years, and he sends me referrals to this day.
