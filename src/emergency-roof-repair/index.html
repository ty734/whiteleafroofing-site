Monsoons, haboobs and hail

Storm damage roof repair in the East Valley

Every summer, roughly June through September, monsoon season throws high winds, driving rain, hail and haboobs at East Valley roofs. Strong winds lift and crack tiles, tear shingles and flashing, and heavy rain finds every weak spot in aging underlayment. Haboob dust and debris clog gutters and valleys so the next storm's water has nowhere to go.

The tricky part: storm damage often isn't visible from the ground, and it may not leak until the next rain. A cracked tile or lifted flashing can sit quietly for weeks, letting small amounts of water weaken the decking underneath. By the time a stain shows on the ceiling, the damage has been working for a while.

That's why the free post-storm inspection matters. I do a full walk-around, check the field, flashing, valleys and gutters, and give you an honest damage report. If your roof came through fine, I'll tell you that and you can stop worrying. If it didn't, we fix it fast, and temporary tarping keeps water out until permanent repairs happen.

Emergency tarping

Roof tarping, and what it actually buys you

When a roof is open and the next storm is coming, tarping is the right call. A tarp goes over the damaged area, gets fastened down so monsoon wind cannot get under it, and keeps water out of the house while the permanent repair gets scheduled. On a tile roof that usually means working around the tiles rather than through them, so the tarp does not create a second problem.

Here is the part the storm-chasers leave out: a tarp buys weeks, not months. Arizona sun degrades tarp material fast, and fasteners work loose in the same wind that caused the damage in the first place. I have seen tarps that were sold as a fix sitting on roofs a year later, quietly leaking. A tarp is a bridge to the repair. It is not the repair.

Tarping is quoted as part of the job rather than sold to you as its own product. If your roof needs covering before it can be properly fixed, that goes into the same bid as the permanent work, and you see both numbers. Most repairs land between $500 and $3,500, and the inspection that produces the number is free.

What counts as an emergency roof repair?

An emergency roof repair is any situation where water is getting in right now, or where the roof is open and the next rain will put water in. Active drips, a ceiling stain spreading during a storm, tiles blown off with the underlayment showing, or a hole from a tree limb all qualify. An old dry stain, scattered debris after a haboob, or a few cracked tiles with no leak are not emergencies, though every one of those is worth a free walk-around before the next monsoon. The table above is the same sorting I would do standing in your living room.

My roof is leaking right now. What should I do?

Move belongings, catch the water in buckets, and if a ceiling bubble is forming, relieve it carefully with a small hole so the drywall doesn't collapse at once. Then call or text me at 480-363-2898 with a photo. Do not climb on a wet roof. The full guide is here: what to do if your roof is leaking.

Can a haboob really damage a roof?

Yes. Haboob winds lift and crack tiles, tear at shingles and flashing, and drive dust and debris into every gap. The damage often isn't visible from the ground and doesn't leak until the next rain, which is why a free walk-around inspection after a big storm is worth doing.
