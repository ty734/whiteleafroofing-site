The process

How a roof insurance claim works, step by step

Make it safe, take photos

Right after the storm, photograph what you can see safely from the ground: missing tiles or shingles, debris, ceiling stains. Date-stamped photos are the backbone of a claim.

Get an honest inspection

Call or text me and I'll walk the roof myself, free. I document everything with photos and a written assessment, and I tell you straight whether the damage is claim-worthy.

Check your policy

Look up your deductible and whether you have actual cash value or replacement cost coverage. If the repair costs less than your deductible, filing usually isn't worth it. I'll say so.

File promptly

If a claim makes sense, contact your insurance company soon after the storm and submit the photos, my assessment, and your notes. Keep a record of every call and date.

Meet the adjuster

Your insurer sends an adjuster to inspect. I can be there for that visit so the real damage gets recorded accurately, and so questions get answered on the spot.

Prevent further damage

Insurers expect you to protect the home while the claim runs. Tarping and temporary repairs are usually covered, and I handle urgent ones fast. See emergency roof repair.

Review the settlement

Compare the insurer's estimate against my bid before you accept. If their number doesn't cover a proper repair, additional documentation can often close the gap.

Repairs done right

My crew does the work, and you get the final invoice and completion paperwork your insurer needs to release the last payment. Then you're done thinking about it.

The part homeowners find out too late

What your policy actually pays

Two homeowners on the same street can take identical monsoon damage and get very different checks. The difference is usually one line in the policy: whether you carry replacement cost or actual cash value. Read that line before you file, not after.

What to look for Replacement cost (RCV) Actual cash value (ACV)
What it pays What it costs to put the roof back today. That same amount minus depreciation for the roof's age and wear.
On a 5 year old roof Little practical difference. Depreciation hasn't had time to bite. Close to the RCV number.
On a 20 year old roof Still pays the real cost of the work. Can fall well short of what the repair actually costs. This is where the surprise happens.
How you get paid Often in two parts: an initial payment, then the held-back depreciation released after the work is finished and invoiced. Usually one payment, and that is the whole of it.
What it means for you Finish the work and submit the paperwork so the second payment gets released. Don't leave it on the table. Budget for a gap between the check and the bid, and get the deductible math done before filing.

Either way, the deductible comes off the top. That's why I'll tell you plainly when a repair is cheaper than filing at all. Most repairs run $500 to $3,500, and you can see how that breaks down in my leak repair cost guide or the Arizona replacement cost guide if the damage is widespread.

Claim questions

What homeowners ask after a storm

Does homeowners insurance cover roof damage in Arizona?

Sudden storm damage, monsoon wind, haboobs, and hail, is typically the kind of event policies cover. Damage from age, wear, or deferred maintenance typically is not. Your own policy controls, so check your deductible and whether you have actual cash value or replacement cost coverage.

Should I even file a claim?

Only if it's worth it. If the repair costs less than or close to your deductible, filing usually doesn't make sense. My inspection is free either way, and I'll give you the honest math before you call your insurer.

Will you meet with my adjuster?

Yes. I can be there when the adjuster inspects so the real damage gets recorded accurately. The coverage decision is theirs; the honest documentation and the quality of the repair are mine.

What if my claim is denied?

You can appeal with additional documentation, and thorough photos and a written assessment from a licensed roofer carry real weight there. For large or complicated claims, an independent public adjuster is another option. And if the denial is legitimate, I'll help you fix the roof affordably instead. Most repairs run $500 to $3,500. I wrote out the whole appeal path in what to do when a roof insurance claim is denied.

How do I file a roof insurance claim in Arizona?

Photograph the damage from the ground with dates, get a licensed roofer to inspect and document it in writing, check your deductible and coverage type, then contact your insurer and submit the photos and the assessment together. Keep a written record of every call and date. Your insurer sends an adjuster, and it helps to have your roofer there for that visit so the real damage gets recorded accurately. The eight steps above walk through it in order.

What's the difference between actual cash value and replacement cost coverage?

Replacement cost pays what it costs to put the roof back today. Actual cash value pays that amount minus depreciation for the age and wear of the roof, so on an older roof the check can be well short of the repair. Which one you have is written in your policy, and it's the single biggest factor in what you end up paying out of pocket. The table above lays out both.

How long does a roof insurance claim take?

Most of the waiting is the adjuster's schedule and your insurer's internal review, and neither of those is yours to control. What you do control is how fast you file and how complete your documentation is, and that's usually what decides whether a claim moves in one pass or bounces back for more information. Meanwhile, protect the house; don't wait on the claim to stop water coming in.

Will filing a roof claim raise my premium?

It can, depending on your carrier and your claim history, which is exactly why the deductible math matters before you file. If the repair costs less than or close to your deductible, filing usually costs you more than it returns. I'll give you that math after a free inspection instead of pushing you toward a claim, because a claim isn't how I get paid.

What should I do while the claim is pending?

Protect the home. Tarping and temporary repairs prevent further damage, are usually covered by your policy, and insurers expect them. If water is coming in now, see the emergency roof repair page or just call me.
